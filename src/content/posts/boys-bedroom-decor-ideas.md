A boy's bedroom usually has all the furniture it needs and still feels unfinished. Bed, dresser, desk — and the room looks like a showroom nobody lives in.

What's missing is layers. Decor is what turns furniture into a room, and it works in a specific order.

## Layer 1: The bed, done properly

The bed is 40% of the visual weight in the room, so it does most of the work.

**Build it in three parts:**

1. **Fitted sheet** — plain, in a colour that coordinates rather than matches
2. **Duvet or comforter** — the main colour statement
3. **One throw folded across the bottom third** — this is the layer that makes a bed look styled rather than made

Add two pillows for sleeping plus one accent cushion. Not five. Boys will not arrange five cushions, and unarranged cushions look worse than none.

**Texture over pattern.** A waffle-weave, chunky knit or linen duvet in a solid colour looks more expensive than a busy printed one, and it doesn't date.

## Layer 2: Texture on the floor

A bare floor makes a room echo and feel unfinished. A rug is the fastest fix.

**Size it right** — this is where people go wrong. The rug should extend at least 18 inches beyond the sides and foot of the bed. For a single bed, that means roughly 5 x 7 feet minimum.

A small rug floating in the middle of the floor makes a room look smaller, not cosier.

**Material:** low-pile wool or a flatweave. Skip shag — it traps everything and can't be cleaned properly.

## Layer 3: Walls at the right height

Blank walls above furniture are what make a room feel temporary.

**Hang art with its centre at 57 inches** from the floor. That's standard eye level and it's the number galleries use.

**Above a bed:** the arrangement should be about two-thirds the width of the bed, hung 6–10 inches above the headboard.

**Options that look considered:**

- Framed prints of his interests — better than posters, and only slightly more expensive
- A picture ledge at 48 inches, so art can be swapped without new holes
- A pegboard painted the wall colour, for medals, caps and gear
- One large piece rather than six small ones — large art makes a small room feel bigger
- A pinboard for the things that would otherwise get taped to the wall

If posters are non-negotiable, get cheap frames. A framed poster reads as decor; a taped one reads as clutter.

## Layer 4: Lighting, three sources

Almost every unfinished-looking bedroom has one overhead light and nothing else.

**Overhead** — general light, ideally on a dimmer.

**Bedside** — a lamp or wall sconce he can reach lying down. Warm bulb, 2700K.

**A third source** — a floor lamp in a corner, a strip light behind a shelf, or a desk lamp. This is the one that makes the room feel layered at night.

A dark corner in a bedroom is what makes it feel unfinished after sunset.

## Layer 5: Something soft at the window

Bare windows or thin blinds leave a room feeling hard.

Curtains do three things: soften the space, block light for better sleep, and add a big block of colour or texture.

**Hang them high and wide** — rod 4–6 inches above the window frame, extending 6–10 inches past each side. This makes the window look larger and the ceiling higher. It's the most common curtain mistake and the easiest to fix.

Blackout lining is genuinely worth it in a child's room.

## Layer 6: Personal things, displayed properly

The difference between a catalogue room and his room is what's on display.

- **Lego builds or models** on a floating shelf, not the windowsill
- **Trophies and medals** on a pegboard or shelf, grouped together
- **His own artwork** in cheap matching frames — this looks genuinely good and costs almost nothing
- **A photo or two** in frames rather than tacked up
- **Books front-facing** on a ledge so covers show

Group things. Five items scattered around the room reads as mess. The same five on one shelf reads as a collection.

## Layer 7: Something living

One plant. A snake plant or pothos survives low light and irregular watering, which is what a boy's bedroom offers.

If that's optimistic, a good-quality faux plant does the same visual job. It softens hard edges and adds a colour nothing else in the room has.

## Storage that doesn't fight the decor

Visible storage is part of the decor whether you plan it or not.

- **Matching baskets** rather than assorted plastic bins
- **Closed storage** for the ugly stuff, open shelves for the good stuff
- **Under-bed drawers** so the floor stays clear
- **One drawer designated for clutter** — the drawer everything gets swept into before people visit

## The 60-30-10 colour rule

If the room's colours feel random, this fixes it:

- **60%** — dominant colour (walls, large furniture)
- **30%** — secondary (bedding, curtains, rug)
- **10%** — accent (cushions, art, lamp, small objects)

Navy walls, cream bedding and curtains, rust cushions and a wooden lamp. Three colours, clearly weighted, and the room reads as designed.

## What to skip

- Full themes on permanent surfaces
- Too many small decorative objects
- Furniture in five different wood tones
- Cool white bulbs
- Rugs that are too small
- Curtains hung at the window frame instead of above it

## The order to do it in

If you're starting from a furnished but flat room, add layers in this order for the biggest impact per pound:

1. Bedding with texture, plus one throw
2. A properly sized rug
3. Curtains hung high and wide
4. A bedside lamp with a warm bulb
5. One large piece of art at 57 inches
6. A shelf for his things
7. A plant

Each step visibly changes the room, and none requires touching the walls or buying furniture.
