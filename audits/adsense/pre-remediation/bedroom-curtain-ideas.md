Curtains are one of the most impactful elements in a bedroom, and the most commonly done wrong. The fabric, the length, and especially the hanging position all affect how large and finished the room feels.

These ideas cover the specific decisions that make bedroom curtains look expensive and intentional rather than afterthought additions.

## 1. Hang the Rod High and Wide

This is the single most important curtain rule. Hang the rod 4–6 inches above the window frame and extend it 6–10 inches past each side of the window. This makes the window look larger, the ceiling feel higher, and the room feel more spacious.

Curtains hung at the window frame make the room look smaller and the ceiling lower.

## 2. Let the Curtains Touch the Floor

Curtains that stop above the floor look unfinished. The most polished options are curtains that just touch the floor, or curtains that pool slightly (1–2 inches of fabric on the floor). Avoid curtains that hover 2–3 inches above the floor.

Measure from the rod to the floor before buying — curtain lengths vary significantly between retailers.

## 3. Add Blackout Lining

Blackout lining improves sleep quality by blocking early morning light. It also adds weight to the curtain, which makes it hang better and look more expensive. It can be added to almost any curtain fabric.

Blackout lining is especially worth adding in east-facing bedrooms that get early morning sun.

## 4. Use Linen for a Relaxed, Natural Look

Linen curtains have a slightly textured, relaxed quality that suits almost every bedroom style. They filter light beautifully and look better as they age. Choose a natural, undyed linen or a warm white for the most versatile option.

Linen curtains wrinkle easily — this is part of their character, not a flaw.

## 5. Use Velvet for a Luxurious Look

Velvet curtains add warmth, texture, and a sense of luxury. They also block light and sound effectively. Choose a deep color — navy, forest green, charcoal, or dusty plum — for the most dramatic effect.

Velvet curtains are heavy — ensure the rod and brackets are rated for the weight.

## 6. Choose a Neutral Color for Longevity

White, cream, warm grey, and natural linen are the most versatile curtain colors. They work with almost every wall color and are easier to update around than a strongly colored curtain.

A neutral curtain also makes the room feel larger than a dark or heavily patterned one.

## 7. Use a Patterned Curtain as the Room's Focal Point

A patterned curtain — a large botanical, a geometric, or a subtle stripe — can be the focal point of a simple bedroom. Keep the rest of the room calm and let the curtains do the work.

Choose a pattern with colors that appear elsewhere in the room.

## 8. Use Eyelet Curtains for a Clean, Modern Look

Eyelet curtains hang in even, regular folds and suit a modern or minimal bedroom. They are easy to hang and slide smoothly on the rod.

Choose a rod that is slightly thicker than the eyelet diameter for the smoothest movement.

## 9. Use Pinch Pleat Curtains for a Tailored Look

Pinch pleat curtains have a more formal, tailored quality than eyelet curtains. They suit a classic or traditional bedroom and look particularly good in velvet or a heavy cotton.

Pinch pleat curtains require more fabric than eyelet curtains — order accordingly.

## 10. Layer Curtains Over Blinds

Layering curtains over a roller blind or Roman blind gives the window both light control and a finished look. Use the blind for daytime privacy and the curtains for evening warmth and style.

Choose a blind in a neutral color that does not compete with the curtains.

## 11. Use Sheer Curtains for Soft Light

Sheer curtains filter light without blocking it, creating a soft, diffused glow. They work well in bedrooms that do not need full blackout but benefit from some privacy.

Layer sheer curtains under heavier curtains for a flexible light-control system.

## 12. Choose a Rod in a Finish That Suits the Room

A brass rod suits a warm, traditional bedroom. A matte black rod suits a modern or industrial bedroom. A natural wood rod suits a Scandinavian or earthy bedroom. The rod is a visible part of the window treatment — choose it deliberately.

Avoid chrome rods in a warm bedroom — they look cold and disconnected.

## 13. Use Tie-Top Curtains for a Casual Look

Tie-top curtains have a relaxed, casual quality that suits a coastal, boho, or country bedroom. They are easy to make from fabric if you want a custom look.

Tie-top curtains do not slide as smoothly as eyelet or pleat curtains — they work best in rooms where the curtains are not opened and closed frequently.

## 14. Add Curtain Holdbacks or Tiebacks

Curtain holdbacks or tiebacks keep the curtains open during the day and add a decorative detail. Choose a holdback in a finish that matches the rod — brass, matte black, or natural wood.

Position the holdback at roughly one-third of the curtain height from the bottom.

## 15. Use Two Panels Per Window

Two curtain panels per window — one on each side — always look more generous and finished than a single panel. Each panel should be at least as wide as the window when closed, so the curtains cover the window fully.

A common mistake is buying curtains that are too narrow — they look skimpy when closed and do not block light effectively.

## Final Thoughts

Bedroom curtains work when they are hung high and wide, reach the floor, and are made from a fabric that suits the room's style. Start with the hanging position — that single change makes more difference than any other curtain decision.
