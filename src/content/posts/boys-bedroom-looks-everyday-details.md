The difference between a bedroom that looks good in photos and one that works on a Tuesday morning is about ten small details. None of them cost much. All of them get skipped.

This is the list.

## The charging station

Phones, tablets, controllers, headphones. Without a designated spot, cables snake across the desk and the bedside table permanently.

**The setup:** a small drawer or a shallow box on the bedside table, one multi-port charger inside, cables fed through a hole drilled in the back.

Devices go in, charge overnight, no visible cables. If you want them out of the bedroom entirely at night, put the station on the landing instead — a decision worth making deliberately rather than by default.

## The laundry system

A laundry basket in the corner is where clothes go to be ignored.

What works better: **a basket by the door**, so clothes get dropped on the way out rather than crossing the room. And a hook for the "worn once but not dirty" clothes that otherwise become a chair pile.

Two baskets is even better — one for lights, one for darks. Sorting at the source saves the argument later.

## The night-before shelf

One small shelf or hook by the door where tomorrow's things live: school bag, PE kit, water bottle, the permission slip.

This single detail removes most of the morning chaos in a house with kids. It works because it takes the decisions out of the morning entirely.

## Bedside essentials

A bedside table needs to hold four things and nothing else:

- A lamp with a switch he can reach lying down
- A water glass or bottle
- Whatever he's reading
- The charging spot

Anything more and it becomes a shelf of clutter. If there's no room for a table, a wall-mounted floating shelf at 26 inches does the same job and leaves the floor clear.

## The one-drawer rule

Every boy's room needs a designated junk drawer. Not a hidden shame — a deliberate one.

Pens, cables, coins, receipts, the bits from a broken toy. Everything that has no home goes there. Empty it every few months.

Without it, that stuff distributes itself across every flat surface in the room.

## Door hooks

The back of the bedroom door is the most underused surface in the house. Three hooks on it hold:

- Dressing gown
- School bag
- The hoodie that lives on the floor otherwise

An over-door rail costs almost nothing and needs no drilling.

## Light switches he can actually use

A bedside lamp with a touch base, a plug-in remote socket, or a smart bulb. Anything that means he doesn't have to get out of bed and cross the room to turn off the main light.

Sounds trivial. In practice it's the difference between the overhead light being left on and not.

## The clock that isn't a phone

A small analogue or LED clock on the bedside table removes the reason to have a phone in the room overnight.

If bedtime routines are a battle, this one small object does more than most rules.

## Blackout, properly

A blackout blind fitted *inside* the window recess plus curtains over the top. Blind alone leaves light leaking round the edges; curtains alone don't block enough.

Children sleep measurably better in a dark room, and in summer this is the difference between 5:30am and 7am.

## A landing spot for pockets

A small dish or tray on the dresser for the things that come out of pockets — coins, cards, the small treasures children collect.

It stops those things being on the floor and it stops them going through the wash.

## Under-bed storage that pulls out easily

Under-bed drawers on castors, not boxes you have to drag on carpet. The difference in whether they get used is enormous.

Use them for out-of-season clothes and the toy rotation, not daily things.

## The seasonal swap

Twice a year, take everything out of the wardrobe and drawers and put back only what fits and is in season. Twenty minutes.

Most children's bedrooms are cluttered not because there's too little storage but because 40% of what's stored no longer fits.

## Cable management on the desk

If there's a desk, three cheap things fix the mess:

- A cable tray screwed under the desktop
- A power strip mounted underneath, not on the floor
- Velcro ties bundling the runs

Ten minutes of work, and the desk looks twice as good in every photo afterwards.

## A place for the collection

Every boy collects something — cards, models, rocks, badges. Give it a designated shelf or box.

Displayed properly it looks like a collection. Spread across the room it looks like mess. Same objects, completely different impression.

**The details, in priority order:**

1. Blackout blind plus curtains
2. Hooks on the back of the door
3. Laundry basket by the door, not the corner
4. Night-before shelf
5. Charging station in a drawer
6. Touch-base bedside lamp
7. Under-bed drawers on castors
8. One junk drawer
9. A dish for pockets
10. A shelf for the collection
