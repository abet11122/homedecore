There's a big difference between a coastal bedroom and a beach-themed one. The first feels calm, airy and expensive. The second has anchors on the cushions and a sign that says "Beach: 2 miles."

The good version is mostly about light, texture and a restrained palette. Here's how it's built.

## The palette

Real coastal rooms are far more neutral than people expect. The blue is an accent, not the base.

**Base (60%):** warm white, cream, sand, pale oat. Not bright white — warm off-whites catch light better and feel softer.

**Secondary (30%):** natural wood, rattan, jute, linen. This is where most of the character comes from.

**Accent (10%):** soft blue, sea glass green, or a faded denim. Small doses.

**What to avoid:** navy-and-white stripes everywhere, bright turquoise, primary red. These are what tip a room from coastal into themed.

**Shades worth sampling:** Pale Oak and White Dove (Benjamin Moore), Sea Salt (Sherwin-Williams), Shaded White and Pale Powder (Farrow & Ball).

## Texture does the work

Take the colour away and a coastal room still reads as coastal, because of the materials.

- **Linen** — bedding and curtains. The single most coastal fabric there is.
- **Rattan and cane** — headboard, bedside table, a pendant light, a mirror frame
- **Jute or sisal rug** — the base layer
- **Weathered or limed wood** — driftwood tones rather than orange pine
- **Cotton in a waffle or gauze weave**
- **Woven baskets** for storage
- **Ceramic and stoneware** — matte, not glossy

Five of these in one room and it reads coastal without a single blue thing.

## The bed

**Headboard:** rattan or cane is ideal. A whitewashed wood frame, or a linen upholstered headboard in oatmeal, works just as well.

**Bedding:** white or cream linen. Slightly rumpled is the point — crisp and pressed reads as hotel, not beach house.

**Layers:** duvet, a lightweight cotton or waffle throw folded across the bottom third, and cushions in cream, sand and one soft blue.

**Skip:** shell prints, anchor motifs, anything with a starfish on it.

## Windows and light

Coastal rooms are defined by light more than anything else.

**Sheer or light linen curtains** — the look is light filtering through, not blocking it. Hang them high, near the ceiling, and floor-length.

**Woven or bamboo blinds** — texture plus light control, and very coastal.

**White shutters** if you have them.

If the room is dark, add a large mirror opposite the window. Light is the whole aesthetic, and a dark coastal room just looks beige.

## Flooring

- **Pale wood** — whitewashed oak, limed timber, or a light engineered board
- **Jute or sisal rug** as the base
- **A soft flatweave layered on top** in cream or a faded pattern

Dark floors fight the aesthetic. If you're stuck with them, a large pale rug covers most of the problem.

## Walls

Keep it quiet. Coastal rooms are calm.

- **One large piece of art** — an abstract seascape, a horizon line, a photograph of water. Muted tones, not vivid.
- **A round rattan mirror**
- **Shiplap or beadboard panelling** painted warm white — genuinely coastal, adds texture
- **Framed botanical or grass prints**
- **Dried grasses** in a ceramic vase

One or two of these. The empty wall is part of the style.

## Lighting

- **A rattan or woven pendant** — instantly sets the tone
- **Ceramic or wooden table lamps** with linen shades
- **Warm bulbs, 2700K**

Avoid chrome, glass globes and anything shiny. Natural materials throughout.

## Plants

Coastal-appropriate greenery keeps it fresh:

- Olive tree in a large pot
- Palm — kentia or parlour palm
- Eucalyptus stems in a vase
- Dried pampas or bunny tails
- A trailing pothos on a shelf

Terracotta, stoneware or basket planters. Not plastic.

## The cliches to skip

These are what turn coastal into kitsch:

- Shells in glass jars
- Rope and anchor motifs
- Signs with beach-related words on them
- Starfish anything
- Bright turquoise
- Navy and white stripes on every surface
- Ships' wheels, oars, lighthouses

One or two natural objects — a piece of driftwood, a single large shell on a stack of books — is charming. A collection of them is a gift shop.

## Storage that fits

- Woven baskets under the bed and on shelves
- A whitewashed wooden chest at the foot of the bed
- A rattan or cane bedside table
- Open shelving with linen boxes

Natural materials again, so storage becomes part of the look.

## Coastal on a budget

The style is cheap to achieve because it favours simple, natural things:

- White or cream bedding from anywhere
- A jute rug is one of the cheapest rug types
- Thrifted baskets and wooden bowls
- Dried grasses last for years
- Paint an existing dresser in warm white
- Cane webbing to update a plain headboard

---

**The formula:** warm white walls, linen bedding in cream, rattan headboard, jute rug, woven blinds, one large muted seascape, warm bulbs, and one soft blue cushion.

Not a shell in sight, and it will read as coastal from the doorway.
