An apartment patio is often 6 x 8 feet of bare concrete with a railing. It's also the only outdoor space you have, so it's worth doing properly.

Here's how to make it usable, starting with what actually fits.

## Measure and plan the zones

Most apartment patios are 30–80 square feet. That's enough for one function, done well — not three done badly.

**Pick one:**

- **Dining** — a bistro table and two chairs
- **Lounging** — two chairs and a small side table
- **Green space** — plants, one chair, a stool

Trying to fit a dining set, loungers and a garden onto 50 square feet is why most small patios end up unusable.

**Leave 24 inches of walkway** to the door.

## Furniture that fits

**Bistro set** — a 24–30 inch round table with two chairs. Needs about 6 x 6 feet including pull-out room.

**Folding furniture** — folds flat against the wall when you need the floor. Best for very small balconies.

**Bench against the railing** with a small table — uses the perimeter, leaves the middle clear.

**Balcony rail table** — a narrow shelf that hooks over the railing. Takes zero floor space.

**Stackable stools** — seating that stores in a corner.

**Materials that survive outdoors:** powder-coated aluminium, teak or acacia, resin wicker. Avoid untreated softwood and untreated steel.

## Flooring over concrete

Bare concrete is what makes a patio feel like a fire escape. Covering it is the biggest single change.

**Interlocking deck tiles** — wood or composite squares that click together over concrete. No adhesive, no damage, and you take them with you. The best renter option.

**Outdoor rug** — polypropylene, which handles rain and dries fast. Cheap and instant.

**Artificial grass tiles** — soft underfoot, good if you have a dog.

Check your lease. Some buildings prohibit covering balcony drainage.

## Privacy

Usually the main complaint about an apartment patio.

- **Balcony privacy screen** — fabric or reed, cable-tied to the railing. Cheap and effective.
- **Bamboo or reed fencing** attached to the rail
- **Tall planters with grasses** — living screen, no attachment needed
- **An outdoor curtain** on a tension rod, if there's structure to hang from
- **A trellis with climbing plants** in a large pot

Check building rules first — many have restrictions on what can be attached to railings or visible from outside.

## Lighting

The thing that makes a small patio usable in the evening.

- **Solar string lights** — no wiring, no socket needed. The most popular option for good reason.
- **Solar lanterns** on the floor or a table
- **Battery LED candles**
- **Clip-on rail lights**
- **A solar-powered floor lamp**

Warm white, not cool. String lights zigzagged overhead or along the railing transform the space at night.

## Plants in containers

Everything on a patio lives in a pot, which changes what survives.

**Easy and tough:**

- Lavender — loves sun and dry soil
- Rosemary and thyme — useful and hardy
- Geraniums
- Succulents in a sunny spot
- Boxwood in a large pot for structure
- Ornamental grasses for movement and screening

**For shade:** ferns, hostas, impatiens, begonias.

**Container rules:** drainage holes are essential, pots dry out much faster than ground soil (daily watering in summer), and bigger pots are far more forgiving than small ones.

**Vertical growing:** a wall planter, a tiered plant stand, or railing planters. Uses no floor space.

**Weight check:** wet soil is heavy. Large planters on a balcony need to respect the load limit — check with your building if you're planning several big pots.

## Storage

- A storage bench — seating plus a box for cushions
- A slim deck box against a wall
- A waterproof bag for cushions
- Hooks on the wall for tools and a folded chair

Cushions must come inside or into a box, or they'll be mouldy within a month.

## Adding comfort

- **Outdoor cushions** in a weather-resistant fabric
- **A throw** for cool evenings
- **A small side table** — even a stool works
- **A rug** to define the seating area
- **A wind chime or lantern** for character

## Shade

If the patio gets direct sun, it'll be unusable at midday.

- A clamp-on balcony umbrella that fixes to the railing
- A shade sail, if there are fixing points
- A tall plant positioned to block afternoon sun
- An outdoor curtain that draws across

## Small details that matter

- **A doormat** at the door
- **A bin** for the small rubbish that accumulates
- **A watering can** — carrying a jug from the kitchen gets old fast
- **A saucer under every pot** so runoff doesn't stain the concrete or drip on neighbours below

That last one causes more neighbour complaints than anything else on a balcony.

---

**The starter setup for a 6 x 8 foot patio:**

Interlocking deck tiles, one bistro set, a privacy screen on the railing, solar string lights overhead, three large planters, and a storage bench.

That's a complete patio, nothing permanent, and it all comes with you when you move.
