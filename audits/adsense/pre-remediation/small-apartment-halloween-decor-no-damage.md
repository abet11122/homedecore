Decorating a small apartment for Halloween has two constraints: limited space and a lease that prohibits damage. Both are solvable. The ideas below are all renter-friendly — no drilling, no permanent adhesive, no damage to walls or surfaces.

The key is to work with the space you have rather than trying to fill it. One strong focal point is more effective than seasonal objects on every surface.

## Walls Without Drilling

### 1. Removable Adhesive Hooks

Removable adhesive hooks — the kind that peel off cleanly — let you hang wreaths, garlands, and lightweight prints without drilling. Use them on doors, walls, and the backs of cupboards.

Test on a small area first if you are unsure about your wall surface.

### 2. Paper Bat Wall Arrangement

Cut-paper or card bats attached with removable adhesive strips create a striking wall display that costs almost nothing and removes cleanly. Arrange them asymmetrically for a more editorial look.

### 3. Removable Wall Decals

Halloween-themed removable wall decals — spiders, webs, bats, moons — apply and remove without damage. Use them on a single wall rather than covering every surface.

### 4. Washi Tape Frames

Washi tape in black or dark orange can be used to create simple geometric frames on a wall, with a Halloween print or card inside. It removes cleanly and costs almost nothing.

### 5. The Tray Method

A tray on the coffee table or console contains the seasonal display and makes it easy to move when you need the surface. A candle, a small pumpkin, and one dark object — a ceramic skull, a small lantern — is enough.

A tray is useful because the whole group can be moved at once.

### 6. Windowsill Display

A windowsill is one of the best spots for Halloween decor in a small apartment — it is visible from outside and does not take up any floor or surface space inside. A small pumpkin, a candle, and a few dried leaves are enough.

### 7. Bookshelf Vignette

Rearrange one shelf of the bookcase to create a Halloween vignette — a few dark books, a small pumpkin, a candle, and one seasonal object. Leave the other shelves as they are.

### 8. Bathroom Counter Display

A small pumpkin, a dark candle, and a skull soap dispenser or dish on the bathroom counter adds a seasonal note to a room that is often overlooked.

### 9. String Lights on a Tension Rod

A tension rod in a doorway or window frame lets you hang string lights without drilling. Warm orange or amber lights create an immediate Halloween atmosphere.

### 10. Fairy Lights in a Glass Jar

A handful of warm fairy lights in a glass jar creates a glowing lantern effect on any surface. It takes thirty seconds and costs almost nothing.

### 11. Swap to an Amber Bulb

Replacing a standard lamp bulb with a warm amber one changes the entire mood of the room without adding a single object. It is also the most reversible change on this list.

## Textiles

### 12. Swap a Cushion Cover

A dark velvet or linen cushion cover in charcoal, deep plum, or forest green adds seasonal depth without any obvious Halloween imagery. Swap it back in November.

### 13. Add a Dark Throw

A chunky knit throw in charcoal, rust, or deep burgundy adds seasonal warmth and changes the mood of the sofa without any other changes.

## Doors and Entryways

### 14. Wreath on the Door

A Halloween wreath hung on the front door with a removable adhesive hook adds a strong seasonal statement that is visible from the hallway. Choose a wreath that suits your door colour.

### 15. Door Curtain

A lightweight black or dark fabric panel hung over the inside of the front door with a tension rod or removable hooks creates a dramatic entryway effect that removes cleanly.

## Scent and Atmosphere

### 16. A Seasonal Candle

A candle in a seasonal scent — pumpkin spice, dark amber, or cedarwood — adds an atmospheric layer that no visual decoration can replicate. One candle is enough for a small apartment.

### 17. Dried Herb Bundle

A bundle of dried sage or rosemary tied with dark ribbon and hung on a removable hook adds a subtle herbal scent and a slightly witchy quality. It also looks good on a hook near the door.

## Final Thoughts

Renter-friendly Halloween decor is about working with what you have and choosing removable solutions for everything else. Start with the tray display and the lighting, add one wall element using removable strips, and keep the palette restrained. A small apartment decorated with restraint looks more considered than one covered in seasonal products.
