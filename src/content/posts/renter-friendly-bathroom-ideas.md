Rental bathrooms are usually the worst room in the flat — dated tile, a dim bulb, a plastic shower rail and beige everything.

Almost all of it can be improved without a drill, and reversed on the day you move out. Here's what actually works, and what to check first.

## Before you start

**Read your lease.** Most allow removable changes; some prohibit adhesive products entirely.

**Take photos of everything** before you change anything, so you know what you're restoring.

**Test adhesives on a hidden patch first.** Peel-and-stick products can pull paint off in badly painted rentals. A small test square behind the door tells you in 48 hours.

**Keep the originals.** The old shower curtain rail, the light shade, the toilet seat. Box them and put them back when you leave.

## 1. Peel-and-stick floor tile

The biggest transformation available and one of the cheapest.

Vinyl tiles go straight over existing tile or vinyl. Patterns available range from checkerboard to encaustic-style. A small bathroom floor costs very little to cover.

**Important:** they work best over smooth surfaces. Heavily textured or deeply grouted tile needs a levelling step, or the pattern telegraphs through. And in a wet bathroom, water getting under the edges is the main failure point — seal the perimeter with removable caulk.

## 2. Peel-and-stick wall tile

For a splashback behind the sink, or a full accent wall. Same rules apply — smooth surface, sealed edges.

Avoid using it inside a shower. Constant direct water will get behind it.

## 3. Change the shower curtain and rail

The single fastest upgrade.

- A **curved tension rod** adds noticeable elbow room in a small shower — no drilling
- A **fabric curtain** with a separate liner looks far better than a plastic all-in-one
- **Extra-long curtains** hung high make the ceiling feel taller

Hang the rod as high as you can. Same principle as windows.

## 4. Swap the light fixture

Often a plastic dome held on by two screws. Ten minutes to change, and you keep the original in a box.

**If you'd rather not touch wiring:**

- Change the bulb to 2700–3000K with CRI 90+ — this alone changes how the room feels
- A battery-operated LED sconce beside the mirror
- A small plug-in lamp on the vanity if there's a socket

## 5. Add a mirror

Rental mirrors are usually small, unframed and badly placed.

- **Lean a large mirror** against the wall on the vanity
- **Frame the existing mirror** with adhesive trim or a peel-and-stick frame kit
- **Hang a mirror on a hook** rated for the weight — one small hook, one small hole

## 6. Storage with no holes

- **Freestanding over-toilet étagère** — straddles the toilet, needs no fixing
- **Leaning ladder shelf** for towels
- **Tension shower caddy** in the corner
- **Tension rod** under the sink for spray bottles
- **Over-door hooks** for towels and robes
- **Adhesive hooks** — check the weight rating and test on a hidden patch
- **A rolling cart** in any gap
- **Baskets** on the floor and on the cistern

## 7. Replace the toilet seat

Nobody thinks of this and it makes a surprising difference in an old bathroom. A new seat is inexpensive, takes ten minutes, and instantly makes the toilet look newer.

Keep the old one.

## 8. Cover what you can't change

- **A bath mat or runner** over dated flooring
- **A shower curtain** hiding a tired tub surround
- **Removable window film** for privacy and to hide poor glass
- **A large plant** in front of an awkward corner
- **Baskets** covering an ugly shelf

## 9. Soft additions

Rental bathrooms are hard, cold and beige. Textiles fix that instantly and come with you.

- Towels in a colour — the biggest single change for the money
- A textured bath mat
- A woven basket
- A rug outside the bath

Buy good towels. They're the thing you touch every day and they move house with you.

## 10. Small hardware swaps

- Replace the shower head — a five-minute change, and rental shower heads are usually terrible. Keep the old one.
- Swap cabinet knobs
- Add a magnetic strip inside a cabinet
- Change the toilet roll holder if it's freestanding

## 11. Plants

Humidity suits pothos, ferns and spider plants. A trailing plant on top of the cistern or a shelf softens the hardest room in the flat.

Eucalyptus tied to the shower head lasts a few weeks and makes the room smell wonderful.

## What to avoid

- Painting without permission
- Permanent adhesive products
- Anything requiring plumbing changes
- Drilling into tile — cracks are expensive and hard to hide
- Peel-and-stick inside a wet shower

## The move-out reversal

Keep a box labelled with the flat's original fittings: light shade, shower head, toilet seat, curtain rail.

Peel-and-stick products come off best with gentle heat from a hairdryer, worked slowly from one corner. Do it well before your inspection, not the morning of.

---

**The highest-impact renter changes, in order:**

1. Bulb swap — 2700–3000K, CRI 90+
2. New towels and bath mat in a real colour
3. Curved tension rod and a fabric shower curtain
4. New shower head
5. Freestanding over-toilet storage
6. New toilet seat
7. Peel-and-stick floor, if the surface suits it
8. A plant

The first five cost very little between them and take one afternoon.
