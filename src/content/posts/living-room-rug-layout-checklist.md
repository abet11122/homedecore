A rug can look generous in a shop and become a small island beneath your coffee table at home. The useful measurement is the footprint of your seating group: sofa, chairs and the space people actually use between them. This guide gives you a way to compare layouts before committing to a purchase. The photographs are illustrative stock interiors, not demonstrations of a tested installation.

## Start with the furniture in its everyday position

Put the sofa and chairs where you normally use them. Pull out a recliner, open the nearby cupboard and move any nesting tables into their usual positions. A rug selected for a temporarily tidy photograph may interfere with how the room works.

Write down the width of the sofa, the outside-to-outside width of the seating group, and the distance from the sofa front to the farthest chair legs. Sketch the doorway and main walking route on the same sheet. You do not need a scaled drawing; labeled measurements are enough to compare available sizes.

## Compare these three arrangements

| Arrangement | What to check | Main trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Front legs of seats on the rug | Each seat reaches the rug by a similar amount | Useful for linking a group without covering most of the floor |
| All furniture legs on the rug | The rug extends beyond the group on every side | Requires more floor area and may be harder to move for cleaning |
| Rug inside a compact seating group | The exposed border looks deliberate and the rug stays stable | Can make separate pieces feel less connected |

These are visual options rather than rules. An asymmetrical room may need an offset arrangement to keep the route to a balcony or kitchen clear.

## Mark two candidate footprints

Use loose paper strips or a sheet folded to size to outline each candidate. If you use tape, first confirm that it is suitable for the floor finish and test it discreetly. Remove the markers after the comparison so they do not become a trip hazard.

For example, compare a 160 × 230 cm rectangle with a 200 × 300 cm rectangle. These are sample footprints, not a recommendation for every sofa. Photograph each from the doorway, then sit down and look at the visible border around the coffee table. Check whether chair legs sit awkwardly across an edge.

## Test the details that a floor outline cannot show

An outline does not reproduce thickness. Check the rug and underlay specifications against the clearance beneath inward-opening doors. Consider whether a raised edge crosses your normal walking route. Choose an underlay compatible with both the rug and your flooring; follow the manufacturers' care and installation instructions.

If furniture is heavy, plan how you will place the rug before delivery. Do not assume a large rug will be easy to pull beneath a fully loaded sofa.

## Keep a short buying note

Record your preferred footprint, acceptable alternatives, door-clearance constraint and care needs. Also note the return conditions before ordering. If both sizes work, choose based on how much floor you want visible and the practicalities of cleaning, rather than assuming bigger is always better.
