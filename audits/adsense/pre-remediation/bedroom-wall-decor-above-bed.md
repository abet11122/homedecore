The wall above the bed is the most visible surface in the bedroom. It is what you see when you walk in, and it is what makes the room feel finished or unfinished. A blank wall above the bed is the most common reason a bedroom looks like a showroom.

These ideas cover the approaches that work consistently — from a single large print to a layered gallery wall — with practical guidance on sizing and placement.

## 1. Hang One Large Piece of Art

A single large canvas or framed print centered above the bed is the simplest and most effective approach. The art should be roughly two-thirds the width of the bed — about 40 inches for a double, 50 inches for a king.

Hang it 6–10 inches above the headboard with the center at 57 inches from the floor.

## 2. Create a Symmetrical Gallery Wall

Two or three matching frames arranged symmetrically above the bed create a clean, hotel-like look. Use the same frame style and a consistent color palette for the prints inside.

Measure and mark the positions with painter's tape before drilling.

## 3. Use an Asymmetric Gallery Arrangement

An asymmetric arrangement of different-sized frames feels more personal and relaxed than a symmetrical one. Mix prints, photographs, and small objects in frames of different sizes.

Lay the arrangement out on the floor first to find a composition that works before hanging anything.

## 4. Mount a Large Mirror

A large mirror above the bed reflects light and makes the room feel larger. Use a mirror with a simple frame in a material that suits the room — wood, metal, or rattan.

Ensure the mirror is securely fixed to wall studs — a large mirror above a bed must be properly anchored.

## 5. Add a Textile Wall Hanging

A woven wall hanging, a macramé piece, or a large piece of fabric adds texture and warmth that framed art cannot. It is also lighter than a framed canvas and easier to hang.

Choose a piece that is roughly the same width as the headboard.

## 6. Use a Picture Ledge

A picture ledge mounted above the bed lets you lean prints and small objects without drilling multiple holes. It also makes it easy to swap art without redecorating.

Use two ledges at different heights for a layered effect.

## 7. Add a Set of Three Prints

Three prints in matching frames, hung in a horizontal row, create a clean and balanced arrangement. Use prints with a consistent color palette — the same two or three colors across all three.

Space them evenly — about 2–3 inches between frames.

## 8. Mount a Decorative Plate Collection

A collection of decorative plates in complementary colors and patterns creates an organic, layered arrangement. Mix sizes and hang them in a loose cluster rather than a rigid grid.

Use plate hangers rated for the weight of each plate.

## 9. Use a Large Botanical Print

A large botanical print — a single oversized leaf, a floral illustration, or a pressed plant print — adds a natural, calm quality to the bedroom. Use a simple frame that does not compete with the print.

Black frames on white walls and natural wood frames on warm walls are the most versatile combinations.

## 10. Add Floating Shelves With Objects

Two floating shelves above the bed — one slightly wider than the other — create a layered display area for small plants, candles, books, and objects. This adds depth that flat art cannot.

Keep the shelves light and uncluttered — three to five objects per shelf is usually enough.

## 11. Use a Neon or LED Sign

A neon or LED sign above the bed adds a personal, playful element. Choose a word, phrase, or simple shape. Use warm white or soft amber rather than a bright primary color for a bedroom.

This works best in a room with a simple, neutral palette where the sign can be the focal point.

## 12. Create a Pressed Flower or Botanical Display

A series of small frames containing pressed flowers or botanical specimens creates a delicate, personal display. Use matching frames and arrange them in a loose grid.

This works particularly well in a bedroom with a natural or earthy color palette.

## 13. Add a Wicker or Rattan Wall Hanging

A large wicker or rattan wall hanging adds texture and a natural quality. It works well in bedrooms with a coastal, boho, or earthy aesthetic.

Choose a piece that is at least 24 inches wide so it reads clearly above the bed.

## 14. Use a Tapestry

A large tapestry hung above the bed adds color, pattern, and texture in one piece. It is one of the most affordable ways to fill a large wall area.

Use a curtain rod or a wooden dowel to hang it so it lies flat against the wall.

## 15. Leave the Wall Intentionally Minimal

In a room with strong color, pattern, or texture elsewhere, a blank wall above the bed can be the right choice. A single small object — one framed print, one small shelf — is sometimes more effective than a full arrangement.

The goal is always a room that feels considered, not a wall that is simply filled.

## Final Thoughts

The wall above the bed works best when the arrangement feels connected to the bed rather than floating above it. Start with the sizing — two-thirds the width of the headboard — and the height — 6–10 inches above the headboard. Everything else follows from those two decisions.
