Boho is the most popular style for girls' bedrooms right now, and it's also the easiest to get wrong. The look depends on layering — and layering is one step away from clutter.

The rooms that work follow a fairly tight set of rules underneath all that apparent looseness.

## The palette

Boho reads as warm and earthy. Get the colours right and everything else falls into place.

**Base (walls):** warm white, cream, oat, or a soft clay. Not grey — grey is what makes a boho room feel cold and modern rather than warm and collected.

**Mid tones:** tan, camel, terracotta, rust, dusty rose, olive.

**Accents:** mustard, burnt orange, sage, a deep clay.

**Metals:** brass and aged gold. No chrome.

**What to avoid:** cool greys, bright primaries, pure black, silver. Any of these fight the warmth the style depends on.

## Texture is the whole style

Boho isn't really about colour — it's about how many different textures you can layer without it looking messy. Aim for at least five in the room:

- **Rattan or cane** — headboard, bedside table, mirror frame, a pendant light
- **Macrame** — a wall hanging, plant hanger, or curtain tie
- **Jute or woven rug** — often layered with a smaller patterned rug on top
- **Linen** — bedding and curtains
- **Chunky knit** — a throw at the foot of the bed
- **Wicker baskets** — storage that's also decor
- **Raw or light wood** — bedside table, shelves, a stool

Five textures in a limited palette is what makes boho look collected instead of chaotic.

## The bed

The bed carries most of the look.

**Headboard:** rattan or cane is the signature. A fabric or a simple wooden one works too. Or skip it entirely and hang a macrame piece or a tapestry above the bed instead.

**Bedding:** linen or cotton in cream, oat or a soft terracotta. Waffle weave, muslin, or a subtle stripe.

**Layers:** fitted sheet, duvet, a chunky knit or woven throw folded across the bottom third, three cushions maximum — one large, one medium, one small lumbar in a different texture.

**Canopy:** a sheer canopy or a simple hoop with fabric draped over the bed is very boho and genuinely lovely. Fix it properly to a ceiling joist.

## Rug layering

The layered rug is a boho signature and it's easy: a large jute or sisal rug as the base, a smaller patterned or vintage-style rug on top, positioned at an angle or offset.

**Size the base rug properly** — it should extend 18 inches beyond the sides and foot of the bed. For a single bed that's roughly 5 x 7 feet.

## Plants

Boho without plants looks incomplete. Three to five, at different heights.

**Easy ones that survive a bedroom:**

- Pothos — trailing, tolerates low light
- Snake plant — nearly indestructible
- ZZ plant
- Monstera if there's good light
- Trailing string of hearts in a macrame hanger

Put one tall on the floor, one or two mid-height on a shelf or dresser, and one trailing from a high shelf or hanger. Varied heights is what makes them read as styling rather than decoration.

Good faux plants are fine and nobody will know.

## Walls

- **Macrame wall hanging** above the bed — the single most recognisable boho piece
- **A tapestry or woven wall art**
- **Floating shelves in light wood** with plants, books and small objects
- **A gallery wall** in warm-toned prints — botanical, abstract, or line drawings
- **A round rattan mirror**
- **Dried flowers** — pampas grass, bunny tails, eucalyptus in a ceramic vase

Pick two or three of these, not all six. The wall above the bed and one other wall is plenty.

## Lighting

Boho lighting is warm and soft, never bright and overhead.

- **A rattan or woven pendant** as the ceiling fixture — instantly sets the style
- **Warm fairy lights** strung along a wall or draped over the headboard
- **A bedside lamp** with a linen or rattan shade
- **Candles** in ceramic or brass holders
- **2700K bulbs everywhere.** Cool white destroys the whole look.

## The line between layered and cluttered

This is where boho rooms fail. Three rules keep it right:

**Limit the palette.** Boho tolerates lots of objects only because they're all in the same warm family. The moment a bright blue or a cool grey appears, it looks random.

**Group small things.** Five small objects on one tray reads as a vignette. The same five spread across the room reads as mess.

**Leave breathing space.** At least a third of every surface empty. Boho is layered, not full.

## Storage that fits the style

- Woven or wicker baskets — the perfect boho storage
- A wooden or rattan chest at the foot of the bed
- Open shelving with baskets on the lower levels
- A clothes rail with a linen curtain in front

Storage in natural materials becomes part of the look rather than something to hide.

## Budget-friendly boho

The style is genuinely cheap to achieve because it favours natural, simple materials:

- Thrift the baskets, vases and wooden objects
- Dried pampas lasts years and costs little
- Make a macrame hanging — it's a beginner craft
- Neutral bedding from anywhere works
- Cheap unfinished wood shelves look right as they are

---

**The formula:** warm white walls, rattan headboard, linen bedding in oat and terracotta, layered jute rug, macrame above the bed, three plants at different heights, brass fittings, warm bulbs.

Follow that and the room looks like it took years to collect.
