Autumn decorating goes wrong in one specific way: people buy orange pumpkins and scattered leaves and end up with a room that looks like a shop display for six weeks.

The version that actually looks good is about warmth and texture rather than motifs. It's also cheaper, and most of it stays out all winter.

## The palette

Skip bright orange. The colours that read as autumn without looking like Halloween:

- **Rust and terracotta** — the anchor colour
- **Mustard and ochre**
- **Warm cream and oat**
- **Deep olive or forest green**
- **Chocolate brown and tan**
- **Burgundy** for a richer version

**Three colours maximum.** Rust, cream and olive. Or mustard, brown and cream. More than three and it turns into a jumble.

## Start with the textiles

This is 80% of autumn decorating and it takes twenty minutes.

**Swap the throw.** A chunky knit, wool or boucle throw in rust, cream or olive, draped over the arm of the sofa or folded across the back. The single biggest change.

**Change two or three cushion covers.** Keep the same inserts, just change the covers — cheaper and easier to store. Mix textures: one knit, one velvet, one linen.

**Add a rug** or layer a smaller patterned one over what you have.

**Heavier curtains** if you have a lighter summer set.

Those four changes alone transform the room, and none of them involve a pumpkin.

## Texture over motif

Autumn feels like layers. Add materials rather than decorations:

- Chunky knit
- Velvet
- Boucle
- Wool
- Leather or tan
- Jute
- Woven baskets

A room with five textures in warm tones reads as autumn even with nothing seasonal in it.

## Natural elements that aren't cliché

- **Dried grasses** — pampas, bunny tails, wheat in a tall vase
- **Eucalyptus** — fresh or dried
- **Dried hydrangeas** — beautiful and they last for months
- **A wooden bowl** with acorns, chestnuts or walnuts
- **Branches** in a large floor vase
- **A pot of dried lavender**

These cost almost nothing, many can be gathered on a walk, and they last the whole season.

## Pumpkins, done well

If you want them:

- **White, cream or pale green**, not bright orange
- **Grouped in odd numbers** — three or five, in varying sizes
- **On a tray or in a bowl**, not scattered
- **Real ones** on a mantel or console, in one spot only

One considered group beats pumpkins on every surface.

## Lighting

Autumn is when the evenings get long, and lighting matters more than any decoration.

- **Turn the overhead off** and use lamps
- **Add one more lamp** than you had in summer
- **Warm bulbs**, 2700K
- **Candles** — a group of three pillars on a tray, or tapers in brass holders
- **A fire**, if you have one

A room lit by three lamps and two candles feels like autumn regardless of what's on the shelves.

## The mantel or console

The one place to put a seasonal arrangement.

**Formula:** something tall at one end (branches in a vase, a candlestick), something medium (a stack of books, a small group of pumpkins), something low (a bowl, a tray of candles).

Uneven numbers, grouped in a rough triangle, one-third of the surface left clear.

## Scent

Underrated and it costs very little.

- A candle in cedar, fig, amber or woodsmoke — not artificial pumpkin spice
- Simmering cloves, orange peel and cinnamon on the stove
- Dried eucalyptus

Scent does more for how a room feels in autumn than most of the visual decoration.

## Coffee table styling

- A tray grouping three things
- A stack of two or three books
- A candle
- A small bowl or vase with dried stems
- A throw folded on the sofa arm nearby

Keep two-thirds of the table clear.

## What to skip

- Bright orange anything
- Scattered artificial leaves
- Signs with autumn words on them
- Anything themed on every surface
- Artificial pumpkin-spice scent

The test: would this still look good in November after the seasonal moment passes? If yes, it's the right kind of autumn decorating.

## Storage and the transition to winter

Keep the seasonal items in one labelled box. Autumn textiles in rust, cream and olive carry straight through to winter — you only swap in a few evergreen sprigs and brass candlesticks in December.

That's the advantage of decorating with texture and warm neutrals rather than motifs. One purchase, two seasons.

---

**The twenty-minute autumn refresh:**

1. Swap the throw for a chunky knit in rust or cream
2. Change three cushion covers, varying textures
3. Put dried grasses in a tall vase
4. Group three candles on a tray
5. Add one lamp, warm bulb, and stop using the overhead light

Nothing themed, and the room feels completely different.
