Wicked is the duo costume of the year. Elphaba and Glinda are visually opposite — black versus pink, angular versus soft, grounded versus ethereal — which makes them one of the most striking pairs you can do. The looks are also genuinely achievable without a costume shop.

The ideas below cover both characters individually, the duo build, group options, and budget alternatives.

## Elphaba

### 1. The Core Elphaba Build

All-black clothing from head to toe, green face and neck paint, a wide-brimmed pointed black hat, and black boots. This is the minimum viable Elphaba and it reads immediately. The hat is the non-negotiable element.

### 2. The Accurate Elphaba

A fitted black dress or black corset top with a full black skirt, long black gloves, a dramatic black cape, the pointed hat, and full green makeup from hairline to collarbone. Add a small broom prop for the finishing detail.

### 3. The Budget Elphaba

Black jeans, a black long-sleeve top, a black hat from a costume shop or charity shop, and green face paint. Total cost under fifteen pounds. The green skin is what makes it work — do not skip it.

### 4. Elphaba Makeup

Green face paint applied from the hairline down to the collarbone, blended at the edges. Use a setting spray to prevent transfer. Add dark eye makeup — deep green or black eyeshadow, strong liner — and a dark lip in plum or deep berry.

### 5. Elphaba Hair

Elphaba's hair is dark and worn down or loosely styled under the hat. If your hair is not dark, a simple black wig works well. The hat covers most of the hair anyway.

## Glinda

### 6. The Core Glinda Build

A pale pink or white dress with volume — a prom dress, a bridesmaid dress, or a tulle skirt with a fitted top — a small crown or tiara, a wand, and an updo. The silhouette is the priority: full skirt, fitted bodice, pale colour.

### 7. The Accurate Glinda

A pale pink ball gown or bubble dress, a sparkly tiara, a star-tipped wand, white or pale pink gloves, and an elaborate updo with curls. Add a beauty mark above the lip for the finishing detail.

### 8. The Budget Glinda

A thrifted pale pink or white dress, a tiara from a pound shop, a star wand made from card and a wooden dowel, and any updo. The silhouette matters more than the specific dress.

### 9. Glinda Makeup

Soft and luminous — a glowing base, pink blush, a light pink or nude lip, and a subtle shimmer on the eyelids. Add a small beauty mark above the lip. The look is polished and pretty, not dramatic.

### 10. Glinda Hair

An updo with volume and soft curls. A high bun with loose curls framing the face is the most recognisable option. Add the tiara at the crown.

## The Duo

### 11. The Classic Duo

Elphaba in full black with green makeup; Glinda in full pale pink with tiara and wand. The contrast does all the work. Stand together for photos and the costumes read from across the room.

### 12. The Elevated Duo

Commission or buy higher-quality versions of both costumes — a structured black gown for Elphaba, a proper ball gown for Glinda. Add matching accessories and coordinate the makeup for a more polished result.

### 13. The Casual Duo

Elphaba in black jeans, black top, hat, and green face paint; Glinda in a pink midi skirt, white top, and tiara. Both looks are comfortable enough for a full evening and easy to move in.

## Group Options

### 14. Add the Wizard

A third person in a green suit or waistcoat with a top hat and a slightly pompous expression completes the core trio. The Wizard's look is more interpretive than the two leads.

### 15. Add Doctor Dillamond

A fourth person in a grey or white outfit with goat-like accessories — ears, a simple horn headband — adds a recognisable supporting character that fans will appreciate.

### 16. The Full Oz Group

Assign each person a character from the show — Elphaba, Glinda, Fiyero, the Wizard, Nessa. Coordinate the colour palettes so the group reads as a unit even to people who do not know the show.

## Alternatives

### 17. Gender-Swapped or Non-Binary Wicked

Both looks translate across genders. Elphaba's silhouette works in trousers and a structured black jacket; Glinda's works in a pink suit with a tiara. Keep the core colour and the signature prop — hat or wand — and the costume reads regardless.

## Final Thoughts

Wicked costumes work because the two characters are so visually distinct. The contrast between Elphaba's black and Glinda's pink is the whole costume — get that right and everything else is detail. Start with what you already own in those colour families, fill the gaps with thrifted pieces, and do not skip the green face paint.
