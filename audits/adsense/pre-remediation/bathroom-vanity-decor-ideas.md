A bathroom vanity is a small surface that has to hold daily essentials and still look good. Most fail because everything lives on top of it.

The fix is a simple rule about what earns a place on the counter, plus a tray.

## The rule: three things on display

Only three categories belong on a vanity surface:

1. **Something functional and attractive** — a soap dispenser, a toothbrush holder
2. **Something living or natural** — a plant, dried stems, a stone dish
3. **Something soft** — a folded hand towel

Everything else goes in a drawer or a cabinet. Toothpaste, razors, skincare, hairbrushes — none of it earns counter space.

## Use a tray

The single most effective vanity move. A tray groups the things that must stay out into one object.

**Sizing:** roughly one-third the vanity width for a single sink, or one tray per sink on a double.

**Materials that survive a bathroom:** marble, ceramic, metal, sealed wood, rattan. Avoid unsealed wood and anything with a fabric base.

Put on it: soap dispenser, a small plant, a candle. That's it.

The tray also means you can lift everything off in one movement to clean.

## Decant what you display

A branded plastic bottle looks like a product. The same soap in a ceramic or glass pump looks like decor.

- Hand soap and lotion in matching pumps
- Cotton buds and pads in small glass jars
- Bath salts in a jar with a wooden scoop
- Toothbrushes in a ceramic cup, not the plastic one they came with

This is the change that makes a vanity look styled rather than just tidy, and it costs very little.

## Mirror sizing

**Width:** the mirror should be roughly the same width as the vanity, or slightly narrower — never wider.

**Height:** centre it at about 60–65 inches from the floor, or centred on the vanity itself.

**Clearance:** leave 4–8 inches between the top of the backsplash and the bottom of the mirror.

**Shape:** an arched or round mirror softens a boxy bathroom. A large rectangular mirror makes a narrow room feel wider.

## Lighting

Bathroom lighting is usually the worst in the house — one overhead fitting casting shadows straight down onto your face.

**Best setup:** two sconces either side of the mirror at 60–66 inches, which lights your face evenly rather than from above.

**If you only have overhead:** add a small lamp on the vanity if there's a socket, or a battery-operated one.

**Bulb temperature:** 2700–3000K. Warm enough to be flattering, bright enough to see properly.

## What to add for warmth

Bathrooms are all hard surfaces — tile, ceramic, glass, mirror. Softness is what makes them feel finished.

- **A folded or rolled hand towel** in a colour that isn't white
- **A small rug or bath mat** with texture
- **A woven basket** on the floor or a shelf
- **A wooden or stone dish**
- **A plant** — pothos, fern or eucalyptus. Humidity suits them.

## Plants that thrive in a bathroom

The humidity that ruins other things is perfect for these:

- Pothos — trailing, tolerates low light
- Boston fern — loves steam
- Spider plant
- Air plants in a small dish
- Eucalyptus stems hung from the shower head (not a plant exactly, but it smells wonderful and lasts weeks)

## Seasonal touches

A vanity can be refreshed in three minutes:

**Spring** — a small vase of tulips, a light-coloured towel
**Summer** — greenery in a clear jar, a citrus-scented candle
**Autumn** — dried stems, an amber glass dish, a deeper towel
**Winter** — eucalyptus, a brass tray, a warm-scented candle

Keep the tray and the soap dispenser. Change the towel and one small item.

## Under the vanity

Everything you took off the counter has to go somewhere.

- Stacking drawers rather than one shelf
- A U-shaped organiser around the pipe
- Clear containers, one per category
- A tension rod with spray bottles hanging
- Adhesive hooks inside the door

## Common mistakes

- Too many products on display
- Branded packaging left visible
- A mirror wider than the vanity
- Overhead-only lighting
- Nothing soft or living in the room
- A tray that's too small to group anything

## Double vanity

Treat each side as its own zone. One tray each, mirrored arrangement, and a shared item in the centre — a plant, a tray of towels, or a candle.

Symmetry works particularly well on a double vanity. Two matching sconces, two matching mirrors, two matching trays.

---

**The formula:**

Tray + decanted soap pump + small plant + one folded towel in colour + warm sconce lighting.

Five elements, everything else in a drawer, and the vanity looks finished in about ten minutes.
