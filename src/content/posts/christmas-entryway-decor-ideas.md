The entryway is the first thing anyone sees at Christmas, and it's also the space with the least room to spare. Coats, boots and bags multiply in December exactly when you want to decorate.

Here's how to do it without blocking the door.

## The rule for a small entry

**Decorate the walls and vertical surfaces. Leave the floor alone.**

A small entry can't afford a floor-standing tree, a basket of pinecones and a nutcracker beside the door. Everything goes up.

Keep 30 inches of clear walkway. If people are stepping around your decorations while carrying shopping, they'll get knocked over.

## Wreath sizing

The wreath on the door is the main event.

**Standard front door (36 inches wide):** a 24-inch wreath. This is the size most people should buy and most people buy smaller.

**Narrow door (30 inches):** 20–22 inches.

**Double doors:** two 18–20 inch wreaths, one per door.

**Rule of thumb:** the wreath should be about two-thirds the door's width.

**Position:** centred, with the top at about eye level — roughly 55–60 inches from the ground, or centred in the upper half of the door.

**Hanging:** an over-door wreath hanger, a suction hook on glass, or a ribbon looped over the top of the door. No holes needed.

## Garland lengths

Garland is sold by the foot and people always underestimate.

**Around a door frame:** measure both sides plus the top, then **add 50%** for the drape and swag. A standard 36 x 80 inch door frame needs about 16 feet of garland to look full rather than stretched.

**On a stair rail:** the length of the rail plus 50–100% depending on how deep you want the swags.

**On a console or shelf:** the length plus a foot for the trailing ends.

Stretched, sparse garland is the most common Christmas decorating mistake. Buy more than you think.

## The console or entry table

The one surface you have. Use the same formula as year-round styling — three heights — with seasonal contents.

**Tall:** branches with fairy lights, a small potted tree, tall brass candlesticks
**Medium:** a garland laid along the surface, a lantern, a stack of books
**Low:** a bowl of baubles, a tray of candles, dried oranges

Keep the key tray. It still has to function as an entryway.

**One-third of the surface stays clear.**

## Lighting

An entry is often the darkest space in a house, and December is dark.

- **Warm white fairy lights** — always warm, never cool white
- **Battery-operated lights** in a garland, so no cable across the walkway
- **A lantern** with a candle or an LED candle on the console
- **A small lamp** if there's a socket

Battery or plug-in with a timer. Six hours on, eighteen off, so you don't think about it.

## Small-space Christmas trees

If you want a tree in the entry:

- **A slim or pencil tree** — 6 feet tall but only 24 inches wide
- **A tabletop tree** on the console
- **A potted rosemary or bay** clipped into a cone — real, useful afterwards, and it looks lovely
- **A wall tree** — branches or lights arranged in a tree shape on the wall, taking zero floor space

## Natural materials

The most reliable way to look elegant rather than plastic:

- Fresh eucalyptus and pine
- Dried orange slices
- Cinnamon sticks tied with twine
- Pinecones
- Bay and rosemary sprigs
- Cranberries or seeded eucalyptus for red

Fresh greenery lasts about three weeks indoors. Mist it and keep it away from radiators.

## Colour schemes that look considered

Pick one and stick to it:

- **Green, brass and cream** — classic, warm, works with any interior
- **Green, white and silver** — cool and crisp
- **Green, rust and dried orange** — warm and natural
- **All white and green** — Scandinavian, calm
- **Traditional red and green**, but muted rather than bright

Two colours plus greenery is plenty for a small space.

## The practical December problem

Coats and boots double in winter. Decorating an entry that's already overflowing makes it worse.

Before you decorate:

- Move out-of-season coats elsewhere
- Add a boot tray if you don't have one
- Add temporary hooks if guests will need somewhere for coats
- Clear the drop zone

An entry that's beautifully decorated and impassable isn't a success.

## Doormat and step

- A seasonal doormat — the cheapest festive change there is
- A lantern or a small potted evergreen beside the door, if there's outdoor space
- Lights around the door frame outside

## Scent

The entry is where people form their first impression. A cedar, pine or orange-clove candle just inside the door, or a simmer pot in the kitchen that carries through.

---

**A complete small entry, in order:**

1. 24-inch wreath on the door, centred at eye level
2. 16 feet of garland around the door frame, with battery fairy lights
3. Console styled in three heights, one-third clear
4. A lantern with an LED candle
5. A seasonal doormat
6. Clear the coats and add a boot tray

Nothing on the floor, nothing blocking the door, and it's the first thing anyone sees.
